Currency differences in group reports

Why a group with a foreign subsidiary has a translation reserve, how Bliss carries it, and where currency effects show up in each report.

When the euro moves, the group's euro company is worth more or fewer kronor. That gain or loss is the translation reserve.

It adds up year after year in equity, while share capital stays at what was put in and each year's profit stays at the rates it was earned at.

1 · The idea

It's like holding a euro savings account

💶

You put 1,000 kr into a euro account when €1 = 10 kr. Later €1 = 14 kr, and the account is worth 1,400 kr.

You never put in 1,400 kr. You put in 1,000 kr and gained 400 kr on the currency. A good report keeps those two apart, forever: what you put in and what the currency did.

K3 and IFRS both ask for exactly that: the group's currency gain is kept separate in equity, it builds up over the years, and it's only booked as profit when the company is sold.

Under K3 it never touches the income statement

K3 30.12 sends the translation difference straight to equity. The income statement shows the year's profit only; the difference shows as the Translation reserve row on the balance sheet.

5 · How Bliss carries it

Each subsidiary's equity, walked forward year by year

  1. A starting pointThe acquisition date, or the first year of books Bliss holds. Equity starts at that day's rate.
  2. Contributions keep their rateA share issue stays at the rate on the day it was made, in every later report.
  3. Profit keeps the rates it was earned atWhen a year closes, its result moves into retained earnings at those rates.
  4. The rest is the translation reserveAssets and liabilities sit at the closing rate; what that adds on top of equity is the reserve, and it accumulates.
  5. The same answer for any periodThe balance sheet on a date is the same whichever month the report starts in.

Every equity figure opens in the drill-down as native amount × rate, labelled Historical rates. The income statement and cash flow don't depend on this, so they read only their own period.

7 · Limits

What isn't covered yet

  1. History older than the synced booksThe reserve starts at zero on the first year Bliss holds. A starting figure from the last audited report would close that gap.
  2. Statement of changes in equityK3 wants the year's translation difference as its own row there.
  3. Selling a subsidiaryThe reserve isn't yet moved into profit on disposal.
  4. Part-owned subsidiariesMinority owners' share of the reserve isn't split out.

Every foreign entity is translated by one rule set: flows at the day's rate, assets and liabilities at the closing rate, equity at historical rates.

The gap those rates leave is the Translation reserve — on the balance sheet only, as K3 requires.

The rates

Which rate each figure uses

Day's rateEvery income and expense, every cash flow, every equity movement (share issue, dividend).
Closing rateAssets and liabilities, including goodwill and surplus values from an acquisition.
Historical ratesEquity balances, carried from the acquisition date or the first year Bliss holds.
Opening rateOnly opening cash in the cash flow, to measure the rate effect on cash.
